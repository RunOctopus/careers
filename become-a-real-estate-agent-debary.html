DeBary is a suburban, family-oriented city of about 23,200 people in west Volusia County, sitting along the St. Johns River between DeLand and Orange City. Its defining feature is the DeBary SunRail station, a commuter-rail stop that puts the greater Orlando job market within a direct train ride, drawing residents who want a lower-cost, more spread-out community without giving up an Orlando paycheck. Here’s exactly how to get licensed, and what the local market looks like for a new agent.

How do you get a real estate license in DeBary?

You earn the Florida real estate sales associate license, valid statewide. There is no separate DeBary license. Be 18+ with a high school diploma, complete the 63-hour pre-license course, get fingerprinted, apply to the DBPR, pass the state exam (75% to pass), and activate under a broker. Most people finish in two to four months. Full detail is in our Florida licensing guide.

Is DeBary a good market for a new real estate agent?

It’s a smaller, more specific market rather than a high-volume one. DeBary itself is home to about 23,200 people, so it won’t generate the raw transaction count of Volusia County’s bigger cities on its own. What it does offer is a distinct buyer profile: commuters who work in Orlando and want the SunRail connection without Orlando’s price tag, plus families drawn to its quieter, more spread-out feel along the St. Johns River. As part of Volusia County, DeBary also draws on the same countywide activity, roughly 900 homes sell each month at about a $343,000 median. For a new agent, understanding that SunRail-driven relocation demand, and being able to speak to it credibly with a buyer, is a real edge in this market.

DeBary Hall Historic State Site and the town’s founding story

Frederick DeBary, a wine importer for one of Europe’s leading champagne houses, built DeBary Hall in 1871 as a hunting retreat along the St. Johns River. The 8,000-square-foot Italianate home became a destination for wealthy sportsmen throughout the late 1800s and early 1900s, drawn to the cool springs and fishing waters. Today, the restored 10-acre historic site remains on the National Register of Historic Places and anchors DeBary’s identity as a place with genuine depth, not just suburban sprawl. For an agent, that story matters: buyers choosing DeBary consciously often appreciate that the town has a founding narrative and a preserved landmark to show for it, separate from the beach towns to the east.

SunRail and the 35-minute Orlando commute advantage

DeBary’s defining infrastructure is the SunRail station, part of an $800 million regional commuter-rail investment that runs from DeBary through downtown Orlando to southern Orange County. Weekday service from the DeBary station reaches downtown Orlando in 35 to 40 minutes, making it the northern gateway to Orlando’s job market. That changes the buyer profile entirely: instead of choosing DeBary for local jobs, buyers are choosing it for affordability, less density, and a direct rail connection to six-figure salaries in downtown Orlando or Seminole County. An agent who understands and can articulate that advantage to a relocation buyer, and contrast DeBary’s rail-linked affordability against Seminole and Orange County prices, has a real, repeatable conversation, not a generic pitch.

St. Johns River Estates and a waterfront real estate niche

DeBary’s St. Johns River Estates neighborhood features custom-built, upscale homes sited between the St. Johns River and Lake Konomac, with many properties offering water views and direct outdoor recreation. These are not the same as rental-focused or starter-home markets; they’re built for buyers who want suburban space, waterfront value, and natural scenery within a commutable distance to Orlando. Like agents who specialize in historic district properties, DeBary agents who develop expertise in riverfront and lakefront homes, understand riparian rules, erosion patterns, and seasonal water fluctuations, build a specialized skill set that general agents in flatter, inland subdivisions simply don’t need. That’s a real market edge.

DeBary Town Center and the town’s active development phase

Immediately adjacent to the SunRail platform, the DeBary Town Center is a transit-oriented mixed-use development approved for over 500 apartments, ground-floor retail, a hotel, and restaurant space. Phase 1 was under active construction in 2024 and 2025, signaling real growth beyond the town’s existing single-family core. For a new agent, this matters: DeBary isn’t a static market. Buyers relocating for that Orlando job via SunRail aren’t choosing between yesterday’s housing; they’re choosing between the established neighborhoods and a genuinely new mixed-use downtown. That development creates new conversations with renters considering ownership, downsizers from older family homes, and young professionals pricing out of urban Orlando.

Does the brokerage you start with matter?

More than the town you pick. Your license must be held by a broker, and that brokerage decides your training, tools, and support in the make-or-break first year. A recognized local name carries real weight, and sellers trust it before you say a word.
