Is the Florida Real Estate Exam Harder Than Other States' Exams?

Home›Become a Real Estate Agent in Florida›FL Exam vs. Other States

Updated July 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

There’s no clean answer, because states test different content with different passing thresholds, which makes a direct comparison hard. Florida’s exam covers national real estate principles plus Florida-specific law, and requires a 75% passing score. The better frame isn’t harder or easier, it’s different, and how well you prepare matters far more than which state’s exam you happen to be taking.

Key takeaways
  • Comparing exam difficulty across states is hard because content, question counts, and passing thresholds all differ.
  • Florida's exam blends national real estate principles with Florida-specific law, and requires a 75% passing score.
  • A tougher-sounding pass rate in one state doesn't necessarily mean a harder test; it can reflect different content emphasis.
  • Preparation quality, especially how well you retain Florida-specific law, predicts your result more than the state you're licensed in.
  • Worrying about which state has the 'hardest' exam is time better spent studying the actual Florida content you'll be tested on.

Why this question doesn't have a clean answer

Aspiring agents researching Florida licensing often land on forums and prep-course blogs asking some version of: is Florida's exam one of the hardest in the country? The honest answer is that it’s very hard to compare directly. Every state writes its own exam, pulls from different content pools, sets its own number of questions, and picks its own passing threshold. A lower reported pass rate in one state might reflect a genuinely tougher test, or it might reflect a state with less rigorous pre-licensing education, a different test-taker population, or simply a different exam structure. None of that tells you much about your own odds of passing in Florida.

What's actually on the Florida real estate exam

Florida's sales associate exam is built in two parts. Roughly a third of the questions cover national real estate principles and practices, the kind of general concepts (contracts, agency, finance, appraisal, property types) tested in some form in most states. The remaining, larger portion covers Florida-specific law: state license law, Florida Real Estate Commission (FREC) rules, disclosure requirements, and how Florida statutes apply to real-world transactions. That combination is exactly why comparisons to other states break down. You’re not just being tested on generic real estate knowledge, you’re being tested on how Florida specifically regulates the profession, which no other state's exam covers in the same way.

Florida's passing score: 75%

Florida requires a 75% passing score on the state licensing exam. Some states set their threshold lower, some set it higher, and some structure their exam into separate national and state sections that each need to be passed independently. A 75% threshold sounds demanding on its own, but on its own it doesn’t tell you whether Florida's exam is harder than, say, a neighboring state's test with a 70% threshold but more obscure content. The threshold is only one variable in a test that has several.

How other states' exams compare

If you look across state licensing boards, you’ll find real differences: some states require fewer pre-licensing education hours before you can even sit for the exam, some use third-party testing vendors with reputations for particular question styles, and some report notably lower first-attempt pass rates than others. It’s tempting to treat a lower pass rate as proof of a harder exam, but pass rates are shaped by many factors beyond raw difficulty, including how well local pre-licensing courses prepare candidates and how much state-specific law a candidate has to absorb. Because Florida requires substantial coverage of its own statutes and FREC rules on top of national content, it’s reasonable to say Florida's exam is thorough, but “thorough” and “hardest in the country” aren’t the same claim, and no consistent national data lets you rank all fifty states against each other with confidence.

Why “harder” is the wrong frame

The more useful way to think about it: Florida's exam is different, not necessarily harder. It tests a real, specific body of Florida law that a candidate in another state simply never sees, and it expects you to apply it accurately, not just recognize it. That’s a different kind of challenge than a generic multiple-choice knowledge test, but different doesn’t automatically mean more difficult. Reframing the question this way matters because worrying about a difficulty ranking you can’t actually verify is time you could spend learning the material that’s actually going to be on your test.

What actually determines whether you pass

Across every state, the strongest predictor of passing isn’t which state's exam you’re taking, it’s how well you completed your pre-licensing education and how deliberately you reviewed before test day. In Florida, that means genuinely learning the Florida-specific law sections rather than skimming them, since that’s the content most likely to feel unfamiliar if your prep leaned heavily on general real estate concepts. Practice exams that mirror the real question style, a realistic study schedule, and treating the state-law portion as seriously as the national portion consistently matter more than any comparison to another state's pass rate.

Bottom line

Florida's real estate exam combines national principles with a meaningful dose of Florida-specific law and a 75% passing threshold, but there’s no reliable, apples-to-apples way to crown it harder or easier than another state's test. It’s different, and your actual result depends far more on the quality of your preparation than on which state issued the exam. Focus your energy there.
