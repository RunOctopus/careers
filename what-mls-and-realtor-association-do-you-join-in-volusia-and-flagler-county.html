What MLS and Realtor Association Do You Join in Volusia and Flagler County?

Home›Become a Real Estate Agent in Florida›MLS & Association by County

Updated August 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

It depends on where your office is. Volusia County is split across three separate local Realtor associations: the Daytona Beach Area Association of Realtors along the northeast coast, the West Volusia Association of Realtors inland along the St. Johns River corridor, and the New Smyrna Beach Board of Realtors in the southeast corner of the county. Flagler County has its own, the Flagler County Association of Realtors. Three of those four (West Volusia, New Smyrna Beach, and Flagler County) are shareholder members of Stellar MLS, the statewide system that grew out of what used to be called My Florida Regional MLS. The Daytona Beach Area Association runs its own separate system instead, commonly called the Daytona MLS. Which one applies to you almost always follows the location of the office you work out of, not your home address, and Adams, Cameron & Co. can walk you through exactly which membership fits your situation.

Why This Question Comes Up for Almost Every New Agent Here

Ask a new agent studying for the state exam which MLS they will use once they're licensed, and most assume there is one answer for the whole region. In Volusia and Flagler counties, there isn't. Volusia County alone is served by three separate local Realtor associations, each with its own membership, its own board of directors, and, as you'll see below, not even the same underlying MLS system. Flagler County has its own association again. That's not typical. In most Florida counties, one county maps to one local board and one MLS, and a new agent studying for their exam anywhere else in the state can usually assume a single, obvious answer. Here, which association and MLS actually apply to you comes down to a much simpler question than most new agents expect, but one that genuinely needs a direct answer rather than a guess: where is your office?

The Short Version, Before the Details

Four separate associations cover this two-county market: Daytona Beach Area Association of Realtors, West Volusia Association of Realtors, and New Smyrna Beach Board of Realtors inside Volusia County, plus the Flagler County Association of Realtors on its own. Three of those four run on the same statewide MLS backbone. One doesn't. Which association you personally join depends on where your office sits, not on which county you happen to live in, and a brokerage with offices spread across this whole footprint, like Adams, Cameron & Co., is genuinely useful precisely because it can tell you which one applies to your specific situation instead of leaving you to piece it together from four different association websites.

Daytona Beach Area Association of Realtors: the Northeast Coastal Corridor

The Daytona Beach Area Association of Realtors traces back to 1922 and covers the northeast coastal part of Volusia County: Daytona Beach, Ormond Beach, Port Orange, Ponce Inlet, Daytona Beach Shores, Holly Hill, and South Daytona. An agent working out of an office anywhere along this stretch of coastline will most likely join this association. Its members carry its own local designation and, importantly, its own separate MLS system, which is covered in more detail below, since it doesn't work the way the other three associations do.

West Volusia Association of Realtors: the Inland St. Johns River Side

On the inland side of the county, along the St. Johns River corridor, the West Volusia Association of Realtors covers DeLand, Deltona, DeBary, Orange City, Lake Helen, and Pierson. This association's roots as a listing-sharing organization go back to 1963, when its board first set up a multiple listing system of its own. Today, membership runs through Stellar MLS, the same statewide system used by New Smyrna Beach and Flagler County (more on what that actually means below). An agent working out of a DeLand or Deltona office will typically join West Volusia rather than the coastal Daytona Beach association, even though both sit in the same county.

New Smyrna Beach Board of Realtors: the Southeast Corner

The New Smyrna Beach Board of Realtors has served the southeast part of Volusia County since 1945, covering New Smyrna Beach, Edgewater, and Oak Hill. For decades it ran its own local system, then joined Stellar MLS as a shareholder in September 2023, folding its listing data into the same statewide backbone that West Volusia and Flagler County already used. An agent working out of a New Smyrna Beach or Edgewater office will generally join this board rather than either of the other two Volusia associations.

Flagler County Association of Realtors: Palm Coast, Flagler Beach, and Bunnell

Flagler County has its own association, the Flagler County Association of Realtors, covering Palm Coast, Flagler Beach, and Bunnell. It joined Stellar MLS as a shareholder in 2022, with full access to Stellar's listing platform live by that summer. An agent working out of a Palm Coast office joins this association, a completely separate membership from any of the three Volusia associations, even though Adams, Cameron & Co. operates offices in both counties under one brokerage.

The One Detail That Actually Matters: Which MLS System You're On

Here's the part that trips up a lot of new agents, and even some experienced ones moving into the area from elsewhere. Stellar MLS is the modern name for what used to be called My Florida Regional MLS, one of the largest MLS systems in the country, which rebranded in 2019. West Volusia Association of Realtors, New Smyrna Beach Board of Realtors, and Flagler County Association of Realtors are all shareholder members of Stellar MLS today, meaning their listings all live on the same statewide backbone and their members can search across it. The Daytona Beach Area Association of Realtors is the outlier. It runs its own separate system, commonly branded the Daytona MLS, built on different software from Stellar's platform. Daytona Beach Area does maintain paid reciprocal listing arrangements with Stellar and several other Florida MLS systems, letting a member cross-post a listing into those systems for an extra fee, but that's a paid add-on, not the same thing as one shared login with full built-in access. Practically, this means an agent whose office works primarily along the coast, but who also lists or shows property inland toward DeLand or south toward New Smyrna Beach, may genuinely need to think through more than one membership, and it's worth asking your managing broker directly rather than assuming.

What MLS Membership Actually Gets You Day to Day

None of this is paperwork for its own sake. MLS membership is the actual infrastructure a working agent depends on every day. It's how you enter a new listing so every other agent in the market can find it, and how you search for homes that match what your own buyers want. It's typically tied to a showing scheduling system, so other agents can book showings on your listings without a string of phone tag. It's usually tied to lockbox and key access as well, the physical or electronic system, commonly a Supra-type box, that lets a showing agent get into a vacant property. And it feeds the comparable sales and market statistics tools agents use to price a listing or advise a buyer on an offer, along with the public-facing search sites that pull their data straight from these local MLS feeds. Board membership itself is also what allows you to use the trademarked title Realtor, a separate thing from simply holding a Florida license. Skip MLS access, and you're working with a fraction of the information everyone else around you has.

What Does It Actually Cost?

Realistically, expect a few layers of dues rather than one flat number: an application or entrance fee, local association dues, Florida Realtors state dues, National Association of Realtors dues, and a separate MLS access fee on top of those. Exactly what each of those costs, and how they're structured, varies by association and changes over time, so this isn't a place to guess at a dollar figure. Confirm current dues directly with whichever specific association applies to your office, and with your brokerage about what, if anything, it covers or reimburses. A new agent should get these numbers in writing before assuming what their real first-year cost looks like.

Your Association Usually Follows Your Office, Not Your Address

Here's the practical rule that resolves most of the confusion: which association and MLS apply to you almost always follows the location of the office you hang your license with, not where you personally live. An agent who lives in DeLand but works out of a Daytona Beach office will typically end up in the Daytona Beach Area Association and its own MLS, not West Volusia, because the office's coverage area is what drives the membership, not the agent's home address. The same logic applies at the county line: an agent working out of a Palm Coast office joins Flagler County's association, even if they grew up in, or still live in, Ormond Beach.

It also matters if your office sits near one of the internal boundaries described above, since Port Orange, for instance, gets listed as part of the Daytona Beach Area Association's coverage in some references and appears in New Smyrna Beach Board of Realtors materials in others. That's exactly the kind of edge case worth confirming directly with your managing broker rather than assuming from a map, and it's exactly the kind of detail a brokerage with offices across both counties can walk you through firsthand.
