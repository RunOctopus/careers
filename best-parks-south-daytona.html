Best Parks in South Daytona, FL

Updated July 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

South Daytona maintains nine city parks packed into a small, walkable footprint on the mainland side of the Halifax River. Reed Canal Park is the standout, 30 acres with a fenced dog park, a disc golf course, and shell hiking trails around a lake. Riverfront Veterans Memorial Park gives residents real river access with a public boat ramp, kayak launch, and fishing pier, right next to The Courts at Riverfront's lighted tennis and pickleball courts. Blaine O'Neal Park is built around a 3-acre lake with a lit fountain and a full baseball field, and James Park is the city's youth recreation hub with a splash pad and two playgrounds.

South Daytona is a small city, under 3 square miles, but its park system is genuinely more complete than its size would suggest. Nine city-maintained parks cover everything from a 30-acre nature park with a dog run to a fishing pier on the Halifax River. Here's an honest look at the ones worth knowing.

Reed Canal Park

The city's largest and most complete park, 30 acres built around a lake, with shell hiking trails through the nature portion, a disc golf course, a multipurpose play field, a playground, and pavilions with picnic facilities. On the north end is a fenced dog park divided into separate small-dog and large-dog areas, a real, specific amenity for anyone evaluating the area with a dog in the family. The lake itself draws anglers and model sailboat enthusiasts.

Riverfront Veterans Memorial Park

Located on South Palmetto Avenue along the Halifax River, this park was renovated with a public boat launch ramp, a kayak launch, and a fishing pier, along with a playground with swings and rentable pavilions. It's the city's real waterfront amenity, genuine river access rather than just a view of it.

The Courts at Riverfront

Sitting right alongside Riverfront Veterans Memorial Park on South Palmetto Avenue, this facility offers a resurfaced, lighted shared tennis and pickleball court plus two dedicated pickleball courts, open 5 a.m. to 11 p.m. and free to use.

Blaine O'Neal Park

Formerly known as Big Tree Park, this 11-acre park on Big Tree Road is built around a 3-acre lake with a lit fountain and a shell walking path, alongside a regulation-size baseball field with a concession stand, a pavilion, and fishing access to the lake. It's wheelchair accessible with about 100 parking spaces, a real full-service park rather than a pocket green space.

James Park

The city's youth recreation hub on James Street, with three baseball fields, two lighted racquetball courts, a lighted tennis court, two playgrounds, a splash pad, and a shaded picnic area. The park is also home to the city's Youth Activity Center, which runs programs like tumbling, Tae Kwon Do, and youth sports camps. This is the park most relevant to families weighing South Daytona specifically for kids' activities.

Magnolia Park

A smaller neighborhood park on Magnolia Avenue built in 1985, with a fishing pier, a playground, and a picnic area, fully wheelchair accessible. It's a quieter, more residential-scale option than the city's larger parks.

Melodie Park

A 2.6-acre park on Oriole Lane with a tennis court and an outdoor basketball court alongside benches and walking trails, a genuinely active-recreation-focused option in a residential pocket of the city.

Ned Wagner Park

A 1.7-acre community park off Reed Canal Road with a basketball court, a playground, and open green space for multi-purpose use, a straightforward neighborhood park serving the surrounding streets.

Central Park and Park of Honor

Central Park, next to the city's municipal building on Magnolia Avenue, is a small open space with a bike and jogging path, benches, and picnic tables. Park of Honor on Olive Street is a different kind of space entirely, built to recognize community members who've made real contributions to the city, with a pond, a fountain, and native plant gardens. Neither is a destination park, but both add real, walkable green space to the city's residential core.

Why this matters beyond just a nice afternoon

For anyone evaluating South Daytona as a place to live, work, or invest, nine maintained parks in a city this small is a real, tangible quality-of-life signal. It says something concrete about how a small municipality invests in its residential neighborhoods, from a 30-acre dog-and-disc-golf park to genuine boat and kayak access on the Halifax River. That's worth knowing whether you're a prospective resident, a buyer, or an agent building real local expertise in this market.

Park amenities and hours can change. Confirm current details directly with the City of South Daytona Parks and Recreation department before visiting.
