How to Choose a Florida Real Estate Pre-License School

Home›Become a Real Estate Agent in Florida›Choose a Pre-License School

Updated July 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

There’s no single “best” Florida pre-license school. The right choice depends on whether you learn better online and self-paced or live with an instructor, what the school’s real state exam pass rate looks like, what’s actually included in the price, and whether it offers realistic exam-prep practice tests. Compare the full package before you enroll, not just the sticker price.

Key takeaways
  • Every approved Florida school teaches the same state-mandated 63-hour curriculum, but format and support vary widely.
  • Online self-paced courses suit self-starters with a flexible schedule; live or in-person courses suit agents who want structure and real-time answers.
  • Ask any school directly for its state exam pass rate rather than relying on its own marketing claims.
  • Compare what’s actually included, exam-prep practice tests, instructor access, course expiration, before comparing price alone.
  • There’s no single best school, only the best fit for how you actually learn and study.

Why the school you choose actually matters

Every approved Florida pre-license school teaches the same state-mandated 63-hour curriculum, but that doesn’t mean every school delivers it the same way. Before you can even sit for the state exam, you need a completion certificate from a school that’s an approved education provider, so your first real decision is who you trust to get you there. The right school gets you through the material in a format you’ll actually finish, prepares you well enough to pass the state exam on your first try, and doesn’t leave you feeling like you wasted a few hundred dollars. The wrong one can mean re-taking sections, re-scheduling the exam, or losing momentum before you’ve even applied for your license.

Online self-paced vs. live or in-person classes

Most Florida schools offer at least one online self-paced option, and many also offer live virtual classes or in-person sessions. Self-paced online courses let you move through material on your own schedule, which works well if you’re studying around a full-time job or family responsibilities, but it requires real self-discipline since there’s no set class time to show up for and no one following up if you fall behind. Live or in-person courses build in a defined schedule and a live instructor you can ask questions to in real time, which some students prefer for accountability and immediate feedback when a concept doesn’t click on the first pass. Neither format is objectively better; it comes down to how you actually learn and what your calendar looks like over the next few weeks.

State exam pass-rate reputation

Ask any school directly what percentage of their students pass the Florida real estate exam on the first attempt, and how that compares to the statewide average. Reputable schools track this and will share it. A strong pass-rate reputation usually comes down to two things: how well the course actually explains the material, rather than just reading a textbook aloud, and whether the school offers dedicated exam-prep beyond the required coursework itself. Word of mouth and honest student reviews, especially from people who finished the course recently, are worth more here than a school’s own marketing claims.

Price range and what’s actually included

Pre-license course pricing varies, and a lower price isn’t automatically a better deal if it doesn’t include exam-prep materials, practice exams, or instructor access. Before comparing prices, get clear on what’s bundled: is post-course exam-prep included or a separate add-on, is there a set expiration date on your course access, is instructor support included or an extra fee, and what happens if you don’t finish before your access runs out? Compare the full package, not just the number on the landing page, and ask what the refund or extension policy looks like if life gets in the way partway through.

Exam-prep practice tests: worth paying for?

The Florida exam covers real estate math, national principles and practices, and Florida-specific law, and it’s a different skill to answer exam-style multiple-choice questions under time pressure than it is to read a textbook chapter. Courses that include realistic practice tests, ideally with explanations for wrong answers rather than just a score, tend to leave students far more confident walking into the real exam. If a course you’re considering doesn’t include practice testing, budget for a separate exam-prep product, or factor that gap into your decision before you enroll.

Questions worth asking before you enroll

A short list of direct questions can save you from a poor fit: what is your first-attempt pass rate, is that number self-reported or independently tracked, what happens if I need an extension, is there a live person I can reach with a question, and are practice exams included or separate? A school that answers these clearly and specifically, rather than deflecting to generic marketing language, is usually a good sign of how it will treat you as a student.

There’s no single “best” school, only the best fit

Every aspiring agent asks some version of “which school is the best,” and the honest answer is that it depends on you. A self-motivated learner with a flexible schedule might do perfectly well with the cheapest self-paced option. Someone who thrives with structure and live interaction might be better served paying more for an in-person or live virtual class. Read reviews, ask about pass rates, compare what’s actually included, and pick the option that matches how you’ll really study, not the one with the flashiest ad.

After you pass: where you land matters more than where you studied

Whichever school you choose, it only gets you to the exam. What happens after you pass, which brokerage sponsors your license, and what training and mentorship you get in your first year, is what actually shapes your career.
