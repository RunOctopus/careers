How do you get a real estate license in Flagler Beach?

You earn the Florida real estate sales associate license, valid statewide. There is no separate Flagler Beach license. Be 18+ with a high school diploma, complete the 63-hour pre-license course, get fingerprinted, apply to the DBPR, pass the state exam (75% to pass), and activate under a broker. Most people finish in two to four months. Full detail is in our Florida licensing guide.

Is Flagler Beach a good market for a new real estate agent?

Yes. It pairs coastal desirability with county-wide growth. Flagler Beach itself is a smaller, sought-after beach town, while surrounding Flagler County is the sixth-fastest-growing county in Florida, adding about 25,000 residents between 2020 and 2025. That combination, premium coastal property plus a rising tide of new residents, gives a new agent both higher-value deals and steady demand to work.

The 1928 pier: Flagler Beach’s iconic landmark and why agents should know its story

Built in 1928, just one year after the town’s incorporation, Flagler Beach’s fishing pier is the town’s defining landmark and appears on nearly every postcard and tourism photograph since it was built. Over nearly a century, the pier has survived repeated hurricane damage and been rebuilt multiple times. What makes this matter to a real estate agent is that the pier reflects something deeper about Flagler Beach itself: a commitment to preservation and small-town character over transient tourism. When the pier was reconstructed most recently, the town chose to preserve the original 100-foot foundation section closest to shore, retaining a tangible connection to the 1928 structure. Buyers choosing Flagler Beach often choose it specifically because of that character, and agents who understand the pier’s history and the town’s preservation values can speak to why a buyer fell in love with the place.

Flagler Beach as “Florida’s Last Beach Town”: small-town preservation in a county that’s exploding

Unlike Daytona Beach 30 minutes south or St. Augustine 45 minutes north, Flagler Beach has largely resisted the high-rise condos, chain hotels, and carnival tourism that reshaped the rest of the Florida shoreline. Building height restrictions keep most structures to three stories, and the town has maintained an Old Floridian character: hurricane shutters in pastel colors, stucco homes, and single-family residential neighborhoods rather than concrete resort corridors. This identity matters commercially. Buyers looking for a vacation home or a retirement escape to Flagler Beach are explicitly rejecting high-rise resort living. That’s a specific buyer profile, and it creates a different sales conversation and a different marketing angle than a beachfront agent in a boom town would ever have.

The retirement and retiree market: Flagler Beach’s core demographic

Flagler Beach has a median age of 58, with about 32 percent of the population age 65 and older. It hosts the Plantation Oaks active 55+ community and draws retirees seeking a quieter coastal lifestyle. What’s crucial to understand as an agent is that this isn’t just a demographic detail. Retirees have different buying patterns, financing needs, and lifestyle priorities than younger buyers. They often prioritize walkability to the beach and downtown, single-story or easy-access floor plans, mature landscaping, and low-maintenance properties. The buyer asking about proximity to doctors, grocery stores, and community centers isn’t unusual in Flagler Beach. An agent who understands this market has a real, durable competitive advantage over someone treating Flagler Beach like any other Florida beach town.

The second-home and seasonal market: where 28 percent of homes sit vacant

Nearly 29 percent of all homes in Flagler Beach are vacant, among the highest rates in Florida. These aren’t abandoned properties. Most are seasonal second homes owned by people who live elsewhere half the year, or investment properties held for vacation rentals. This creates a distinct real estate market separate from primary-residence sales. Buyers shopping for a vacation home or a retirement retreat have different price points, financing (often cash), timeline flexibility, and decision-making speed than someone relocating their family. Similarly, sellers of second homes have different urgency and negotiating expectations than primary-home sellers. An agent who specializes in the seasonal and second-home market in Flagler Beach has a genuine niche with fewer competitors than you might expect in a six-thousand-person beach town.

Does the brokerage you start with matter?

More than the town you pick. Your license must be held by a broker, and that brokerage decides your training, tools, and support in the make-or-break first year. A recognized local name carries real weight, and sellers trust it before you say a word.
