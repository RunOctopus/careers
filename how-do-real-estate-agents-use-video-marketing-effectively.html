How Do Real Estate Agents Use Video Marketing Effectively?

Home›For Experienced Agents›Video Marketing

Updated July 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

Real estate agents get the best results from video by keeping it simple and consistent: short listing walkthroughs shot on a phone, neighborhood highlight videos that build local authority, and brief talking-to-camera market updates. These formats tend to outperform polished, generic promotional videos, and posting regularly matters more than production quality, especially for a solo agent without a marketing budget.

Key takeaways
  • Short, phone-shot listing walkthroughs (two to three minutes) tend to get watched more fully than long, heavily produced tours.
  • Neighborhood highlight videos build local authority and keep working long after a specific listing sells.
  • Simple talking-to-camera market updates feel more useful and memorable to viewers than polished promotional graphics.
  • Consistency (posting on a regular schedule) matters more than production value, especially for solo agents on a limited budget.
  • Generic self-promotional videos tend to underperform; videos that lead with a home, a neighborhood, or a market fact perform better.

Why video has become part of the job

Buyers and sellers now expect to see a property, and the agent, before they ever pick up the phone. Video gives them both in a few minutes: a walkthrough of the home and a sense of who they’d be working with. For an agent in Volusia and Flagler counties, that’s a fast way to earn trust with someone who found a listing online and hasn’t met anyone from the brokerage yet. It also extends an agent’s reach past their own contact list, since a well-made video can circulate to people who’ve never worked with a real estate agent before but recognize a familiar neighborhood or a home style they like.

Short listing walkthroughs outperform polished tours

A two to three minute phone video walking through the kitchen, the primary suite, and the backyard, narrated in your own voice, tends to get watched all the way through. A long, heavily produced tour with music and slow pans often gets skipped after a few seconds. The winning format is simple: point out what a buyer would actually notice on a showing, mention the details that don’t show up in photos (natural light in the afternoon, a quiet street, storage space), and keep it moving. Ending with a clear, low-pressure next step, like inviting questions or offering a private showing, works better than a hard sales pitch.

Neighborhood highlight videos build local authority

Videos that aren’t tied to a specific listing, a two-minute look at a Daytona Beach neighborhood, a nearby park, or a stretch of restaurants and shops, do something a listing video can’t: they position you as the local expert even when you don’t have anything for sale there yet. These videos hold up for months or years, and they’re often what a relocating buyer finds first when they search a neighborhood name instead of an address. Over time, a small library of these videos across different pockets of Volusia and Flagler counties becomes a real asset, since it keeps drawing views long after any single home has sold.

Simple talking-to-camera market updates work better than they sound

A short, unscripted video where you talk directly to the camera about what’s happening in the local market, inventory levels, how many days homes are sitting, what buyers are asking about, can feel more useful to a viewer than a slick graphic post. It also does more for your reputation: people remember a face and a voice far longer than they remember a chart. You don’t need a script. A few bullet points and a clear opening line are enough, and it helps to answer the same question a client might ask you in person: what should someone buying or selling right now actually expect?

Consistency matters more than production value

A phone-shot video posted every week beats a professionally edited video posted once a quarter. Consistency is what builds the habit of a viewer checking for your next post, and it’s what search and social platforms reward with more visibility over time. For a solo agent without a marketing budget, that’s good news: the barrier to starting is low. A recent phone, decent lighting (near a window works), and a plan to post regularly matter more than equipment. Editing can stay minimal too; a clean cut at the start and end is usually enough, and most agents don’t need more than a basic phone app.

What to avoid: generic promotional content

Videos that are purely self-promotional, "choose me because I’m the best agent," without showing an actual property, neighborhood, or useful piece of information, tend to underperform. Viewers scroll past anything that reads as an ad. The videos that hold attention give the viewer something first: a look inside a home, a fact about an area, an honest read on the market, and let your expertise come through naturally rather than being stated directly. The same logic applies to where a video is posted; a video worth watching tends to perform reasonably well wherever it’s shared, while a purely promotional one struggles everywhere.

Getting started without overthinking it

Pick one format to start, most agents find listing walkthroughs the easiest entry point since there’s already a reason to be at the property, and commit to posting on a regular schedule for a few months before judging results. Keep videos short, keep the phone steady, and talk the way you’d talk to a client standing next to you. Add neighborhood and market update videos once the listing walkthroughs feel routine. Over time, a simple, consistent habit builds a library of content that keeps working long after the video was first posted, and it becomes far easier to maintain once it’s a normal part of listing a home rather than a separate project.
