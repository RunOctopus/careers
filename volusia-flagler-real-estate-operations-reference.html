Quick answer

Four separate Realtor associations cover Volusia and Flagler County, running on two different MLS systems, and which one applies to you follows your office location rather than your home address. The Daytona Beach Area association covers the northeast coast on its own MLS; West Volusia covers the inland St. Johns corridor, New Smyrna Beach Board covers the southeast, and Flagler County covers Palm Coast, Flagler Beach and Bunnell, all three on Stellar MLS. Sign rules are set city by city: unincorporated Volusia allows 4.5 square feet set back 5 feet for three months a year, while Palm Coast allows two signs at 6 square feet each and requires removal within three days of closing.

Key takeaways
  • Four associations and two MLS systems cover two counties. Most Florida counties have one of each, so advice written for the rest of the state is wrong here.
  • The Daytona Beach Area association runs its own MLS. The other three are on Stellar MLS. This is the detail that most often catches out an agent moving in.
  • Your association follows the office your license is registered to, not where you live.
  • Sign rules change at every city line. Unincorporated Volusia allows 4.5 square feet; Palm Coast allows 6 and starts a three-day removal clock at closing.
  • Florida law protects a security-company sign near your door. It does not protect a for-sale sign, so HOA covenants can be stricter than the city code.

What this page is

This is the operational reference for working as a real estate agent in Volusia and Flagler County: which Realtor association and multiple listing service you end up in, and what the local sign rules actually say. It exists because this market is unusually fragmented and no national source gets it right. Most Florida counties map one county to one association and one MLS. This two-county market has four associations and two different MLS systems, and the boundaries do not follow the county line.

Where you can legally put a sign

Sign placement looks like common sense until you read the code and discover it is a patchwork. The two jurisdictions below differ on maximum size, on setback, on how many signs are allowed, on whether an open-house sign is separately permitted, and on how long the sign may stay up. An agent who assumes the rule they learned in one city travels to the next one will be wrong somewhere in this market.

Unincorporated Volusia’s 4.5 square feet applies in the R-1 to R-6 and R-9 residential classifications; other zoning classifications rise to 32 square feet, which is why a highway commercial listing can carry a far larger sign than a house two streets away. Palm Coast permits one additional open-house sign of up to 9 square feet, and caps sign height at 6 feet.

Three rules that hold everywhere here

Stay out of the public right-of-way. That strip along a road belongs to the government, not the homeowner, and it usually extends several feet past the pavement and often past the sidewalk, including grass a homeowner mows and assumes is theirs. Volusia County’s code prohibits signs on public property or rights-of-way and states that a sign erected in violation may be removed.

Do not attach a sign to anything vertical that is not yours. Volusia prohibits signs affixed to trees, shrubbery, vines, utility poles or beach dune walkover structures. Palm Coast prohibits attachment to utility poles, trees and traffic control devices, and separately restricts signs mounted to fences. The utility pole rule is not bureaucratic: staples and nails left in a pole are a real hazard to a line worker putting a hand where your sign used to be.

The HOA layer can be stricter, and in Florida it is allowed to be. Some states protect a homeowner’s right to advertise their own property for sale. Florida does not. Under F.S. 720.304(6) a parcel owner may display a reasonably sized sign provided by a contractor for security services within 10 feet of an entrance to the home. That is the whole protection, and there is no companion provision covering a for-sale sign. Inside a deed-restricted community, the recorded covenants set the real limit.

What joining actually costs

There is no single number, and anyone quoting you one is guessing. Expect several layers rather than a flat fee: an application or entrance fee, local association dues, Florida Realtors state dues, National Association of Realtors dues, and a separate MLS access fee on top. Each is structured differently by association and each changes over time.

We deliberately do not publish figures here. A dues number that is a year stale is worse than no number, because someone will budget against it. Confirm current amounts with the specific association your office falls under, and ask your brokerage in writing what it covers or reimburses before you join.

How current this is, and what is not covered

Association names, coverage areas and MLS affiliations reflect the position at the time of writing and are believed accurate. Associations occasionally merge, rebrand or change MLS partners, so confirm current status directly with the association. The sign table covers two jurisdictions in full and explicitly marks the rest as unverified; it is a starting point for the cities it names and a warning for the ones it does not.
