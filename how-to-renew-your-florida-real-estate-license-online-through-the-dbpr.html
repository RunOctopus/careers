How to Renew Your Florida Real Estate License Online Through the DBPR

Home›Keep Your License Working›Renewing Online

Updated August 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

You renew a Florida real estate license online through the DBPR licensing portal at MyFloridaLicense.com. Set up or log in to your online account, confirm your required education is showing as reported under your license, submit the renewal, and pay the fee. The step that catches people is the education: your course provider reports your completion electronically to the department, not you, and that reporting has its own timeline. Finishing a course the week your license expires is not the same as having it on your record before the deadline.

Key takeaways
  • Renewal is filed in the DBPR online licensing portal at MyFloridaLicense.com. Set the account up before you need it, not the week of your deadline.
  • You do not submit your own course certificates. Approved providers report completions electronically to the department.
  • Providers must report electronically within 10 business days beginning on the 30th day before the renewal deadline, or before your expiration date, whichever comes sooner. Correspondence courses report by the fifth day of the following month.
  • Check that your hours actually appear under your license in the portal. That is the record that counts, not your course confirmation email.
  • Keep a paper copy of every course completion certificate. The department can audit, and the certificate is your proof.

The online renewal itself is genuinely simple, and if that were the whole story this page would be four sentences long. The reason agents run into trouble is that renewal is a two-part process where only one part is under your direct control on the day, and the other part runs on someone else's schedule.

This page covers the online mechanics specifically. For the wider picture of what renewal involves, see how to renew your Florida real estate license.

Where renewal actually happens

Florida real estate renewals are filed through the DBPR online licensing portal at MyFloridaLicense.com. The department encourages online renewal, and it is faster and more traceable than the paper alternative.

The single best piece of preparation is establishing your online account early. Not the week your license expires, not when the renewal notice arrives, but now. Account setup and recovery involve identity verification and can take longer than you would like, and there is no version of that problem that is pleasant to solve against a hard deadline.

The part that is not under your control

Here is the thing most agents do not know until it bites them: you do not report your own education to the state. Approved education providers report course completions electronically to the department on your behalf.

That reporting has a defined schedule. Providers must electronically report a licensee's course completion within 10 business days, beginning on the 30th day before the renewal deadline or before the licensee's expiration date, whichever occurs sooner. Correspondence courses are on a different clock and only need to be reported by the fifth day of the month following completion.

Read that again with a deadline in mind. If you finish a correspondence course in the last week of March and your license expires March 31, the reporting requirement for that course type may not land until the following month. Your course is done. Your record is not.

Why you should verify, not assume

Your course completion email is a receipt from a vendor. Your license record at MyFloridaLicense.com is the state's account of whether you have met the requirement. Those are two different things and only one of them determines whether your renewal goes through.

The department encourages licensees to log in and monitor the continuing education hours reported under their license, and that advice is worth taking literally. Finish your coursework, wait for the reporting window, then go back in and confirm the hours are actually showing. If they are not showing and your deadline is close, that is a problem you want to be solving with weeks in hand, not days.

Separately, keep a paper copy of every course completion certificate. Licensees are required to retain them in case the department audits their education. A vendor's portal is not a substitute for your own file.

What you will need in front of you

Nothing exotic. Your DBPR account credentials, your license number, a payment method, and confirmation that your education has posted. The renewal fee is generally in the range of $60 to $75 depending on license type, and the exact amount owed appears in your account. Our page on what renewal really costs covers the full spend, since the state fee is the smallest piece of it.

Renewing early is free, and it is the whole trick

You can start well before your expiration date, and renewing early does not shorten your next cycle or move your date forward. Your next expiration stays two years out from the current one.

So there is genuinely no cost to being early and a real cost to being late, which makes this one of the rare situations with an obviously correct strategy. Complete your education with a comfortable margin, verify it posted, then submit. Agents who compress all of that into the final fortnight are the ones who find out their package was missing a core law component, or that their provider's reporting had not run yet, with no room left to fix it.

If something is wrong on your record

Two problems come up often enough to name. First, hours not appearing: contact your education provider directly, since they are the party responsible for reporting, and have your completion certificate ready. Second, contact information out of date: fix it while you are logged in. Florida statute requires the department to notify you 90 days before your license expires, and that notice goes to whatever address and email the state has on file. An agent who moved or left a brokerage that was receiving their mail is an agent who never got the only automatic warning in the system.

Renewing while you are between brokerages

Your renewal and your employing broker are separate records. Renew regardless of where you are employed, and do not assume a brokerage transition pauses the obligation or that someone at either office is tracking it for you. A license in good standing transfers cleanly to a new broker, but a lapsed one creates a problem at exactly the wrong moment. If you are moving near your date, our page on switching before or after renewal covers the sequencing.

What happens if you miss it

It depends on which renewal you missed, and the difference is larger than most people expect. Missing continuing education on a later renewal makes your license involuntarily inactive, which is recoverable on a defined path set out in our page on reactivating an involuntarily inactive license. Missing post-license education before your first renewal makes the license null and void under Florida law, which ordinarily means re-qualifying from the beginning. If this is your first renewal, read our page on the 45-hour post-license course before you do anything else.
