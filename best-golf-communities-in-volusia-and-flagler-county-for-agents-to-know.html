Best Golf Communities in Volusia and Flagler County for Agents to Know

Home›Become a Real Estate Agent in Florida›Golf Communities by County

Updated August 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

A handful of named golf communities come up again and again once you start working with buyers in this market. LPGA International in Daytona Beach is a 36-hole, dual-course community built around the LPGA's own home course, with gated residential neighborhoods along both layouts. Plantation Bay, just north of Ormond Beach, is unusual because it actually straddles the Volusia and Flagler county line, offering 45 holes across multiple courses inside one gated community, and it is not age-restricted even though it draws a heavily active-adult buyer. Pelican Bay, near South Daytona, pairs two 18-hole courses with a large gated community that leans 55-plus. Over in Flagler County, Grand Haven and Hammock Beach in Palm Coast both center on Jack Nicklaus-designed courses, with Hammock Beach adding a second course by Tom Watson and an oceanfront resort layer on top of its residential side. Knowing these names, and roughly what each one actually offers, lets you talk credibly with a golf-focused buyer from the first conversation instead of looking them up after the fact.

Why a New Agent Should Know These Names

A meaningful slice of buyers relocating to this part of Florida are looking specifically for golf, whether that's a retiree who wants to play three times a week or a younger buyer who golfed at home and wants to keep doing it. These buyers often arrive already knowing the names of the communities they're interested in, sometimes from a magazine feature, a golf trip years earlier, or a friend who already lives here. If a buyer mentions Plantation Bay or Hammock Beach and an agent has to ask what that is, it undercuts confidence fast. You don't need to be a golfer yourself, and you don't need to have played every course in the region, but you do need to know the real names, roughly where each community sits, and what distinguishes one from another.

LPGA International: Daytona Beach's Namesake Golf Community

LPGA International sits in Daytona Beach and takes its name from the Ladies Professional Golf Association, which relocated its headquarters here in the early 1990s after the city donated land and renamed a stretch of road LPGA Boulevard to help make it happen. The community centers on two 18-hole championship courses, one designed by Rees Jones and the other by Arthur Hills, both built in 1994. Gated residential neighborhoods run along both layouts, giving buyers a range of home styles and price points within a single, well-known address. For a buyer who wants genuine championship-level golf as the centerpiece of daily life, and likes the idea of living inside the LPGA's own home turf, this is usually one of the first names that comes up.

Plantation Bay: the Community That Straddles Both Counties

Plantation Bay is worth knowing specifically because it's the rare community that actually crosses the Volusia/Flagler county line, sitting just north of Ormond Beach and reaching into Flagler County toward Flagler Beach. It's a large, gated, master-planned community built around 45 holes of golf across multiple courses, along with its own country club. Here's the detail that surprises a lot of buyers and even some agents: despite looking and feeling like an age-restricted active-adult community, Plantation Bay carries no legal age restriction at all. It draws a large 55-plus population by reputation and lifestyle, but families with children can and do live there too. That distinction matters, because a buyer who specifically wants (or specifically wants to avoid) an age-restricted community needs to hear this correctly rather than assume based on how the marketing feels.

Pelican Bay: Two Courses Near South Daytona

Pelican Bay is a large, gated community near South Daytona, close to both the beach and LPGA International, built around two separate 18-hole golf courses along with a clubhouse, tennis courts, and a pool. It's widely described as a premier 55-plus community in the area, which makes it a natural fit for a retiree buyer who wants golf, an active clubhouse scene, and a gated setting without being right on the coast. The golf club itself is open to public play in addition to serving residents, which is worth mentioning to a buyer who wants to try the course before committing to a home nearby.

Grand Haven: a Nicklaus Design in Palm Coast

Grand Haven, in Palm Coast, centers on an 18-hole championship course designed by Jack Nicklaus and opened in 1998, laid out with a natural, rolling movement that Nicklaus Design has compared to a low-country Carolinas layout, running along oaks, pines, and views of the Intracoastal Waterway. The course has repeatedly landed on Links magazine's list of Top 100 Courses in North America, which is a genuinely useful fact to have ready for a buyer who wants to know the community isn't just locally well-regarded but nationally recognized. Grand Haven is a gated community with large homesites and its own country club, appealing to a buyer who wants a serious golf course without the resort-hotel layer that comes with Hammock Beach.

Hammock Beach: Golf Plus an Oceanfront Resort

Hammock Beach, also in Palm Coast, is the community to know for a buyer who wants golf and an oceanfront resort lifestyle in the same address. It offers two courses: the Ocean Course, designed by Jack Nicklaus and known for its wetlands, lakes, and coastal views, and the Conservatory Course, designed by Tom Watson and built around waterfalls and natural stonework. Beyond the golf, Hammock Beach functions as a resort community with towers, villas, a full-service spa, and multiple dining options, alongside straightforward residential ownership. It's a different buyer profile than Grand Haven or Plantation Bay: someone who wants the resort amenities and oceanfront setting as much as the golf itself.

Public Play vs. Resident-Only Access

One distinction worth getting right before a buyer asks: not every course in this list works the same way when it comes to who can actually play it. Pelican Bay's golf club is open to public play, meaning a buyer can go try the course before ever putting in an offer nearby, which is a genuinely useful thing to mention to someone weighing a purchase. Hammock Beach's courses operate as part of a resort, with access tied to resort guests, members, and residents rather than open walk-up public play. Grand Haven and Plantation Bay both run their golf as private, member and resident-oriented clubs attached to the community itself, the more typical arrangement for a gated country-club community. None of this is complicated once you know it, but a buyer who assumes every course on this list works like a municipal course they can just show up and play will be surprised, and it's a much better conversation to have before a showing than during one.

Talking About Price Honestly, Without Guessing at Numbers

New agents sometimes feel pressure to have a dollar figure ready the moment a buyer asks about a golf community, and that instinct causes real problems. Pricing across these communities shifts constantly with the broader market, varies enormously by lot size, view, home age, and whether a listing includes a golf membership, and quoting a stale or wrong number to a buyer is worse than not quoting one at all. What's fair and useful instead is relative framing grounded in what these communities actually are: Plantation Oaks-style manufactured home communities sit at the more affordable end of the spectrum generally; established communities like Pelican Bay and LPGA International offer a range of home ages and sizes, which usually means a wider price range within the same gates; and newer, resort-anchored communities like Hammock Beach, along with newer construction inside Grand Haven, tend to sit at the higher end given the amenities and construction age involved. The honest, professional move is to pull current, real listings inside the specific community a buyer is asking about, rather than reciting a number from memory that may be a year or two stale.

What to Actually Do With This List

None of this means memorizing scorecards or course ratings. What matters practically is being able to place each community on the map correctly, in the right county, and describe the one or two things that set it apart from the others: LPGA International's championship pedigree and central Daytona Beach location, Plantation Bay's county-spanning footprint and lack of an actual age restriction, Pelican Bay's 55-plus lean near South Daytona, Grand Haven's nationally ranked Nicklaus design, and Hammock Beach's oceanfront resort layer. When a buyer brings up a name you don't recognize, it's fine to say so and offer to look into it together rather than guess. What builds trust fastest is getting the names and the basic facts right the first time, and being honest on the rest.
