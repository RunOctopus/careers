Daytona Beach vs. Ormond Beach: Where Should a New Real Estate Agent Start?

Home›Become a Real Estate Agent in Florida›Daytona Beach vs. Ormond Beach

Updated August 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

Daytona Beach and Ormond Beach sit five minutes apart in the same county, share the same brokerage options, and even the same county-wide median price, so the real choice isn’t which market is better, it’s which buyer and transaction type fits how you want to work. Daytona runs on higher transaction volume, tourism, and investment-property turnover. Ormond runs on fewer, steadier deals with an older, more established buyer base.

Key takeaways
  • Daytona Beach and Ormond Beach are both in Volusia County and share the same ~$343K county median price, this isn’t a cheaper-vs-pricier decision.
  • Daytona has more transactions and more entry points for a brand-new agent (condos, rentals, first-time buyers), but also more agents competing for them.
  • Ormond Beach has fewer, larger deals, more all-cash and move-up buyers, and a market that rewards patience and relationship-building over volume.
  • Adams, Cameron & Co. operates full-service offices in both markets, so this is a specialization choice you make after joining, not a brokerage decision.
  • Most new agents find their answer by picking the buyer conversation they’d rather have every day, not by picking the “bigger” market.

New agents often frame this as which town is the “better” market. That’s the wrong question here. Daytona Beach and Ormond Beach are both served by the same brokerages, sit in the same county, and share the same countywide median sale price. The real difference is what kind of business each market actually produces, and which one fits the way you want to spend your first year.

Why this isn’t really a “which town is better” question

Daytona Beach and Ormond Beach border each other. A five-minute drive separates most of Daytona’s beachside condo corridor from Ormond’s oceanfront estates. Both fall inside the same Volusia County pricing data, and Adams, Cameron & Co. runs full-service offices in both, with the same training, marketing, and manager support either way. So the decision isn’t about brokerage quality or even affordability. It’s about which day-to-day business you’d rather build.

If you want volume and fast entry points, Daytona Beach fits

Daytona’s economy runs on tourism, motorsports, and a large renter and investor population, which keeps transaction volume high year-round. For a brand-new agent, that means more first-deal opportunities: smaller condos, rental turnover, and first-time buyers priced out of pricier coastal towns. The tradeoff is competition. More transactions also means more agents working the same buyer pool, so you’ll need to move fast and stay visible.

If you want fewer, bigger, more relationship-driven deals, Ormond Beach fits

Ormond Beach’s buyer base skews older, more established, and more likely to pay cash. Its real estate inventory leans toward oceanfront estates and golf-community homes rather than beachside condos, and the town’s history as one of Volusia’s original established communities gives it a steadier, more repeat-client-driven market. It’s a better fit for an agent who wants to build a smaller book of larger, more durable relationships rather than chase volume.

You don’t have to pick one forever

Plenty of Adams, Cameron & Co. agents work both markets, or start in one and shift toward the other as their book of business develops. The brokerage doesn’t change. What changes is which buyer conversation you’re having most days, and that’s worth thinking through honestly before you start, rather than defaulting to whichever town you happen to live closest to.
